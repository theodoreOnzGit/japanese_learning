# Food and Drink

Eating, ordering, describing food and temperature. Seeded from the Fukushima
hotel breakfasts and class vocabulary.

Status labels: `new` · `shaky` · `solid` · `mastered` — see `MAP.md`.

## Language

### Core (N5)

#### つめたい vs さむい — two kinds of cold — `new`

From class 2026-08-01: *"つめたい cold (drinks/something that you touch)."*
Exactly right, and the contrast is the part worth holding:

| Word | Cold what |
|---|---|
| **つめたい**（冷たい） | things you **touch** — drinks, water, hands, metal |
| **さむい**（寒い） | the **air** — weather, a room, how you feel in it |

> つめたいビール ✅ / ~~さむいビール~~ ❌
> 今日はさむいですね ✅ / ~~今日はつめたいですね~~ ❌

Rule: if you could touch it, つめたい. If it's the temperature you're standing in,
さむい.

**Class example, 2026-08-01** — sensei's sentence, **not** the user's production.
Input only; no bearing on status.

> いい天気でした。でも、ちょっと**さむ**かったです。

Weather is ambient → さむい, not つめたい. A useful model sentence for the
distinction, but the user has not yet chosen between さむい and つめたい unaided.

**Extra sense:** つめたい also describes a **cold-hearted person** —
あの人はつめたいです. さむい cannot do this.

**The same split runs on the hot side** — and there both words are あつい, with
the kanji doing the work:

| | Touch | Ambient |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | つめたい 冷たい | さむい 寒い |
| Hot | あつい **熱**い | あつい **暑**い |

熱いお茶 (hot tea, touchable) vs 暑い日 (a hot day, ambient). Add **厚い**
("thick") and that's three kanji for one reading — see `reading_traps.md`.

All four are い-adjectives → past つめたかった / さむかった / あつかった, never
~~つめたいでした~~ (see `describing_and_sentence_building.md`).

#### なし（梨）— pear — `new`

From class 2026-08-01. The Japanese/Asian pear — round, crisp, very juicy, not
the European teardrop shape.

**Homophone trap:** **なし** is also **無し** ("without / none") — 砂糖なし
("no sugar"), 予約なし ("no reservation"). Context separates them, and 梨 is
almost always written in kanji or katakana (ナシ) on price tags to avoid exactly
this. Fifth tracked same-reading pair — see `reading_traps.md`.

Bought at a **八百屋（やおや）** — see `shopping.md` for the 〜屋 shop suffix.

## Cultural

### いただきます / ごちそうさま — `new`

Not yet a logged mistake, but adjacent to material already tracked:
**いただきます** before eating is the same いただく as the humble receiving verb
(`gifts_and_giving.md` → keigo ladder) — literally "I humbly receive." Said even
when eating alone. **ごちそうさまでした** after finishing — note the past form,
same 完了 logic as ありがとうございました (`presenting_and_formal.md`).
