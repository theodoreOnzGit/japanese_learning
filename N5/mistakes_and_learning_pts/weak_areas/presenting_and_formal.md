# Presenting and Formal Speech

Giving a talk, opening and closing, stating opinions, addressing a teacher or an
audience. Built up largely around the Fukushima show-and-tell (`fukushima_trip.tex`).

Status labels: `new` · `shaky` · `solid` · `mastered` — see `MAP.md`.

## Language

### Core (N5)

#### ありがとうございます vs ありがとうございました — `shaky`

Flagged twice on 2026-08-01 — **stated, then misapplied within the same hour**,
which is exactly why the rule isn't yet trusted.

- **ありがとうございます** (non-past) — thanks for something **ongoing or about to
  happen**: a gift being handed over now, help you're receiving, a compliment
  being paid to you right now.
- **ありがとうございました** (past) — thanks for something **completed**: the end
  of a talk, a meal, a lesson, a transaction.

Quick test: **can you still be in the middle of the thing you're thanking for?**
Yes → ございます. Over and done → ございました.

| Situation | Form |
|---|---|
| Being complimented | ありがとうございます |
| Being handed a gift | ありがとうございます |
| End of your own presentation | ありがとうございました |
| Leaving a shop / after a meal | ありがとうございました |

Same 完了 (completed-action) logic as わかりました (see
`everyday_conversation.md`) — Japanese marks the *completedness of the event*
where English says "thank you" either way. Shop staff split it exactly this way:
ございます while serving you, ございました as you leave.

**Presentation bookends:** open with **よろしくお願いします**, close with
**ありがとうございました**.

Miss history: 2026-08-01 (a) wrote ございます to close a presentation;
(b) an hour later wrote ございました to answer a compliment. Both directions
wrong once — the distinction is understood but not yet automatic in either
direction.

#### ご清聴ありがとうございました — presentation-specific closing — `new`

Offered by Gemini (2026-08-06 speaking practice) as an alternative to どうも
ありがとうございました on Slide 12. **ご清聴（ごせいちょう）** = "your
attention/listening" — ご + 清聴 (せいちょう, "listening attentively"), the
honorific ご because it's the *audience's* attention being thanked (see お/ご
prefix rule below). ご清聴ありがとうございました is the standard, slightly more
formal way to close a talk specifically — どうもありがとうございました works too
but is more general-purpose ("thank you very much" for anything).

#### Formal sentence-final か must be explicit — `new`

Flagged by Gemini (2026-08-06 speaking practice): in formal spoken Japanese
(です/ます form), the question particle **か** must be spoken explicitly at the
end (〜でしたか) — don't rely on rising intonation alone the way English
question-marking sometimes allows. This applies throughout Slide 11's Q&A
(福島は危険でした**か**。／どうして福島に行きました**か**。) and any formal
question generally.

#### 自分で (じぶんで, "by myself") — `new`

Used on Fukushima slide 5: 自分で調べたいと思いました ("I wanted to investigate
for myself"). Reused 2026-08-01 for 日本語は自分で書きました ("I wrote the
Japanese myself").

#### AI (エーアイ) / 人工知能 + 使う — `new`

Asked 2026-08-01. **AIを使いました。** — "I used AI."

- **AI is read エーアイ** — Latin letters are spelled out individually, never
  pronounced as a word. Same rule as flight numbers (SQ633 = エスキュー ろく さん
  さん). Not "ai" as in English "eye."
- **人工知能（じんこうちのう）** = "artificial intelligence." Correct but
  formal/news-register; everyday speech uses AI.
- **使う（つかう）** = to use. う-verb: 使います / 使いました / 使って.

> 宿題で AIを使いました。 — "I used AI for my homework."
> スライドを作るのに AIを使いました。 — "I used AI to make the slides."

で marks the *tool*, same as はしで食べます / 電車で行きます.

**Disclosing AI use to a teacher** (2026-08-01) — be precise about *what* it did:

> AIを使いました。でも、日本語は自分で書きました。
> "I used AI. But I wrote the Japanese myself."

Fuller: 日本語は自分で書きましたが、文法をチェック**してもらいました**。
(〜てもらう — see `gifts_and_giving.md`.)

**No すみません needed.** Disclosing a tool honestly isn't an apology; adding
すみません frames it as wrongdoing. Save it for an actual rule break. To ask
permission instead: AIを使ってもいいですか。

#### 思い出 (おもいで) — "memories" — `new`

Vocab, class mode 2026-08-15 (student presentations). Noun — 楽しい思い出
("happy memories"), 思い出になりました ("it became a memory," i.e. a
meaningful/memorable experience).

### Stretch (N4)

#### 〜と思います — marking opinion vs fact — `new`

Self-identified 2026-08-01: "there is a form which talks about, it's my opinion
rather than fact right?" Yes. Already used unknowingly on slide 5
(調べたいと思いました).

**Pattern: [plain form clause] + と思います.** The clause before と must be
**plain form** — this is the trap, since the instinct is to keep the polite
ます/です used elsewhere in the sentence.

| Word type | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| verb | 行く**と思います** | ~~行きますと思います~~ |
| い-adjective | 安い**と思います** | — |
| な-adjective | 危険**だ**と思います | ~~危険ですと思います~~ |
| noun | 学生**だ**と思います | ~~学生ですと思います~~ |

な-adjectives and nouns need **だ** before と. い-adjectives and verbs attach
directly. Only the final 思います carries politeness — everything inside the
quote is plain.

> いいえ、危険じゃなかったと思います。 — "No, I don't think it was dangerous."

危険 is a な-adjective, so plain past negative = 危険**じゃなかった** (never
~~危険くなかった~~, the い-adjective shape).

**Update (2026-08-06, external speaking practice with Gemini):** correctly
produced and explained 危険じゃなかったと思いました unprompted while reading
Slide 11's FAQ answer aloud — identified 危険 as な-adjective and got the
plain past-negative right inside the と思う quote. Good spontaneous
reinforcement of both this entry and the 危険 na-adjective status in
`nuclear_and_science.md`.

**Hedging ladder** (decreasing commitment): 〜と思います ("I think") → 〜でしょう
("probably") → 〜かもしれません ("might").

**When to use it:** measured numbers are fact and can be stated flat
(0.11 μSv/hでした / 飛行機の中でした). "Fukushima is safe" is a judgement and
should be marked as opinion. Stating an opinion as bare fact reads as
overconfident.

#### Honorific prefixes お / ご on nouns — `new`

Flagged 2026-08-01 — the user wrote 質問がありますか to an audience. Correct, but
when the noun belongs to *the listener*, Japanese prefixes it to elevate them:
**ご質問**. This is 尊敬語 on a noun.

| Prefix | Attaches to | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ご** | 音読み (Sino-Japanese) | ご質問、ご家族、ご住所、ご意見 |
| **お** | 訓読み (native Japanese) | お名前、お金、お手紙、お水 |

Rough rule: **音読み → ご, 訓読み → お**. Exceptions exist (お電話, お時間).

**Only for the other person's things.** Your own question is 質問; theirs is
ご質問. Using ご質問 of your own over-elevates yourself — the same misstep
category as あげる aimed at a senior.

Some お have **fused permanently** and are used regardless of owner: お金, お水,
お茶, お店, お風呂. See `shopping.md` → お金.

#### 何か + は for softening a question — `new`

Learnt alongside the above. **何か (なにか, "any/some")** softens an existence
question, and **は** over が topicalises it ("as for questions…"), sounding more
inviting. 何かご質問はありますか is more natural than a bare 質問がありますか,
which can sound abrupt.

**Clarified (2026-08-06, ChatGPT rehearsal):** both **ご質問はありますか**
("Do you have questions?") and **何かご質問はありますか** ("Do you have *any*
questions?") are natural — 何か isn't required, it just adds the "any"
nuance. Also caught in the same rehearsal: never stack が and は on the same
noun (~~ご質問がはありますか~~) — see `describing_and_sentence_building.md` →
が vs は.

## Cultural

### Audience and teacher as 外 (out-group) — `new`

An audience or a customer is 外 and therefore elevated — which is why ご質問 and
the humble/honorific verb forms apply when presenting. Same relational logic as
`gifts_and_giving.md` → Hierarchy dictates verb choice (keigo).
