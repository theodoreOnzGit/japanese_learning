# Gifts and Giving

あげる / くれる / もらう, who gives to whom, and the customs around gifting.
The single densest topic on this list — and the one where language and culture
are least separable, which is why they now sit in one file.

Status labels: `new` · `shaky` · `solid` — see `MAP.md` for definitions.

## Language

### Core (N5)

#### The giving/receiving trio — `shaky`

From class 2026-07-18. **Pattern: [giver は] [person に] [thing を] あげます** —
に marks the recipient, を marks the thing given.

> （私は）友達に プレゼントを あげます。 — "I give a present to my friend."

| Verb | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| あげます | I / someone → outward (away from me) | give |
| くれます | someone → me / my in-group | give (to me) |
| もらいます | I ← from someone | receive |

**もらいます** — the inward verb. Pattern:
**[receiver は] [giver に／から] [thing を] もらいます**. に (or から, a clearer
"from") marks the giver/source; を marks the thing received.

> 友達に プレゼントを もらいました。 — "I received a present from my friend."

**Trap:** もらう allows に→から, but あげる's recipient に **cannot** become から.
から only works with もらう.

#### 私にあげました is ALWAYS wrong — `shaky`

あげる flows *outward, away from the speaker*, so 私 can never be its recipient.
"My friend gave me chocolate" has two correct forms, never あげる:

| | Sentence | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ A | 友達が 私に チョコを **くれました** | くれる = someone gives *toward me* (giver = subject) |
| ✅ B | 私は 友達に チョコを **もらいました** | もらう = *I received* (receiver = subject) |
| ❌ | ~~友達が 私に チョコを あげました~~ | あげる can't point at 私 |

Same event, two viewpoints: くれました from the giver's side, もらいました from
the receiver's side. Memory hook: gift **away from me** → あげる; gift **toward
me** → くれる (their view) / もらう (my view); **私に + あげる = always wrong.**

#### Extends to family / in-group (内 uchi) — `shaky`

The "toward me" boundary is not just literally 私 — it covers your whole
**in-group** (family, people on your side):

- ✅ 田中さんが 母に 花を **くれました** (giver's view)
- ✅ 母は 田中さんに 花を **もらいました** (receiver's view)
- ❌ ~~田中さんが 母に 花を あげました~~ — 母 is 内, あげる can't point at her.

あげる only works when the gift leaves your whole in-group to an outsider, or
passes between two outsiders. See **Cultural → 内/外** below.

#### The double に — `shaky`

に does three different jobs across these verbs. Two often appear in one
sentence:

> わたしは バレンタインデー**に** かれ**に** チョコレートを あげます。
> バレンタインデーに = *time*, かれに = *recipient*.

> 旧正月**に** こども**に** お年玉を あげます。
> 旧正月に = *time*, こどもに = *recipient*.

> わたしは クリスマス**に** カリナさん**に** CDを もらいました。
> クリスマスに = *time*, カリナさんに = ***source*** ("from"), because もらう.

**Minimal pair (class, 2026-07-18)** — same particle, same person, opposite
direction; only the **verb** decides "to" vs "from":

- わたしは クリスマスに カリナさん**に** CDを **もらいました**。 → *from* Karina
- わたしは クリスマスに カリナさん**に** アクセサリーを **あげます**。 → *to* Karina

So に's three jobs: **time** (バレンタインデーに) · **recipient/goal**
(かれに あげます) · **source** (友達に もらいます).

### Stretch (N4)

#### 〜てもらう — receiving an *action* — `new`

Taught 2026-08-01. te-form + もらう = "have someone do something for you." Same
inward direction as plain もらう — the favour comes toward you.

| | Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| noun | 友達に プレゼントを もらいました | received a present |
| action | 友達に 説明**して**もらいました | had a friend explain |

> 文法をチェック**してもらいました**。 — "I had it check my grammar."

#### Keigo ladder — hierarchy changes the verb — `new`

From class 2026-07-18. You **cannot** say あげます to a senior — あげる implies
"doing them a favour" and sounds presumptuous aimed upward. The verb itself
shifts by social level.

- **Giving (me → other), by recipient's status:** やる (juniors/animals/plants)
  → **あげる** (equals) → **さしあげる (差し上げる)** (seniors/customers, humble
  謙譲語). "I give to a teacher" = 先生に … を **さしあげます**.
- **Receiving:** **もらう** (from equals) → **いただく (頂く)** (from a superior,
  humble). 先生に 本を **いただきました**. Same いただく as いただきます before
  eating — "I humbly receive."
- **Other gives to me:** **くれる** (equal/junior → me) → **くださる (下さる)**
  (superior → me, honorific 尊敬語). 先生が … を **くださいました**. Same くださる
  as ください.

One-liner: to a senior → **さしあげる**; from a senior → **いただく**; a senior
gives you → **くださる**. 謙譲語 lowers *your* action; 尊敬語 raises *theirs*.

#### Vocab: 旧正月 / お年玉 — `new`

- **旧正月（きゅうしょうがつ）** — "Lunar New Year." 旧 (きゅう, "old/former") +
  正月 (しょうがつ, "New Year"). Reading note: 正月 = しょう**がつ**.
- **お年玉（おとしだま）** — "New Year's money gift for children." お (polite) +
  年 (とし) + 玉 (だま, rendaku from たま, "ball/gem").

## Cultural

### バレンタインデー / ホワイトデー — `new`

From class 2026-07-18, via わたしは バレンタインデーに かれに チョコレートを
あげます. In Japan the gift direction is **gendered and split across two days**:

- **バレンタインデー (Feb 14):** *women* give *men* chocolate — the reverse of the
  Western default. Two kinds worth knowing:
  - **本命チョコ (ほんめいチョコ)** — "true-feeling" chocolate for a romantic
    interest / partner.
  - **義理チョコ (ぎりチョコ)** — "obligation" chocolate for male colleagues,
    bosses, friends out of social duty (義理 = obligation), no romantic meaning.
- **ホワイトデー (Mar 14):** one month later, *men* reciprocate to women who gave
  them chocolate, traditionally with white sweets (cookies, white chocolate,
  marshmallows). 返し (return gift) is the expectation.

Why it's a weak area: the direction (women→men first) and the 本命/義理 split are
non-obvious to Western learners, and they change what the あげます example
actually *implies* socially.

### お年玉 / 正月 vs 旧正月 — `new`

From class 2026-07-18, via 旧正月に こどもに お年玉を あげます.

- **お年玉 custom:** at New Year, adults/relatives give **children** small
  envelopes of money. The Japanese cousin of Chinese 紅包 (hóngbāo). Direction is
  adult → child, so it fits あげます (outward).
- **正月 vs 旧正月:** plain **正月（しょうがつ）** in Japan = the **Gregorian**
  New Year (Jan 1), the major national holiday, and when お年玉 is given.
  **旧正月（きゅうしょうがつ）** = the **lunar** New Year (春節 style), which most
  of mainland Japan does *not* celebrate (Okinawa and some regions do). So the
  class sentence frames the custom in its lunar-calendar form — natural in a
  Chinese-cultural context, less so as mainstream Japanese practice.

### Giving/receiving verbs encode 内/外 (uchi/soto) — `new`

From class 2026-07-18: "もらいました is a cultural thing." The あげる/くれる/もらう
system is not neutral like English give/receive — the language **forces you to
encode social perspective every time**: who is in your **in-group (内, uchi)** vs
**out-group (外, soto)**, and from whose side you're telling it.

- **English is viewpoint-neutral:** "gave"/"received" say nothing about sides.
- **Japanese is viewpoint-obligatory:** a gift *toward* you or your in-group must
  use くれる/もらう; you cannot describe it with あげる. Family = 内, so it counts
  as "me."
- **Why it's cultural:** 内/外 is a core organising idea in Japanese society — you
  humble your own group and elevate outsiders/superiors. Same relational logic
  drives keigo (below). Choosing the verb is an act of social positioning, not
  picking a synonym.

Practical upshot: when a sentence involves giving or receiving, first ask
**"whose side is each person on, and whose side am I telling this from?"** — that
decides the verb before grammar does.

### Hierarchy dictates verb choice (keigo) — `new`

From class 2026-07-18: "you can't say あげます to a senior." The cultural rule
underneath is that **social hierarchy (先輩後輩, in-group vs superiors,
staff→customer) changes which verb you use**, not just whether you add です/ます.
Politeness in Japanese is *relational* — it encodes who is above and below whom,
so a wrong-level verb is a social misstep, not merely a grammar error.

- **Giving to a superior:** さしあげる, not あげる.
- **Receiving from a superior:** いただく, not もらう (also why meals start with
  いただきます).
- **A superior giving to you:** くださる, not くれる (also the root of ください).

Underlying split: **謙譲語 (humble)** lowers *your own* actions; **尊敬語
(honorific)** raises *the other person's*. You never raise yourself or lower them.
